NSE Circular No. 58/2026 | November 20, 2026

10 Critical Questions:

1. Static IP access is intended only for tech-savvy retail investors who develop their own algos using programming knowledge.

2. Retail traders who depend on third-party algo providers may face restrictions on Static IP access.

3. Does understanding markets, options strategies and risk management no longer suffice? Must every algo trader also become a programmer?

4. What happens to traders using no-code and low-code algo platforms?

5. Will retail traders now need to hire programmers and build their own technology infrastructure?

6. If an algo strategy is developed with professional assistance, how will the trader qualify under the new definition?

7. Will smaller algo providers find it increasingly difficult to serve retail clients?

8. Will compliance costs and regulatory uncertainty discourage retail participation in algo trading?

9. Large institutions have technology teams and infrastructure. How will independent retail traders compete?

10. Most importantly, what is the practical alternative for an ordinary retail trader who understands trading but does not know programming?

MY CONCERN:

Investor protection is absolutely necessary. However, regulation must strike a balance between risk management and accessibility.

Algo trading should become safer, more transparent and more accessible — not a privilege limited to people with programming skills.

SEBI and NSE must clarify how ordinary retail traders can practically access compliant third-party algo solutions under this framework.
