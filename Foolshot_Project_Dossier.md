# Foolshot project dossier

**Puneet Vishnawat · Live experiment design · Revised 24 November 2026 · Indian markets**

**Primary question.** Can an AI improve its later decisions by recording its hypotheses, reviewing outcomes and testing new ideas on future sessions? The [study protocol](docs/research/study-protocol.md) defines the matched paper comparison and the evidence needed for publication.

**Choose Dhan for a 90-calendar-day live experiment with a ₹1,00,000 capital ceiling.** Allow up to one preparatory week for testing the plumbing. Start the 90-day clock on the first scheduled live session after setup, including sessions when the model declines to trade. The model’s job is to observe the live market, read timely news, decide what to do with real capital and learn from actual outcomes. Broker records establish whether any order traded.

Foolshot asks two independent questions. Did the agent earn money after charges and operating costs? Could it handle a real trading account reliably, and how often did a person need to intervene? A losing strategy can still produce an informative operational result. A money-making week with missing records cannot establish autonomous performance.

## What 5% per week means

Score the target as **₹5,000 a week after trading charges on the original ₹1 lakh**, before personal income tax. This keeps the denominator fixed even after withdrawals. Report each full live week separately. Identify partial weeks, outages and cumulative results separately. A flat full live week misses the target, even if staying in cash was the correct decision.

Thirteen full target weeks would produce ₹65,000 with profits swept. Reinvesting every gain would produce about ₹88,565 instead. The 90-day live window can include partial weeks. Actual target-hit counts depend on the start date.

The target conflicts with a small pilot. At ₹25,000 exposure, two round trips per day and five sessions, ten trades must average **₹500 net each**. That is a 2% average gain on each position, including losers. At ₹10,000 exposure, it becomes 5% per trade. Fewer trades raise the hurdle. Keep the target visible. A missed week never increases position sizes or creates a duty to trade.

**Risk sizing example · proposed design.** With a ₹500 share, a ₹496 stop and a ₹10,000 exposure cap, at most 20 shares fit. Their price risk is ₹80. Add estimated round-trip costs and slippage before checking the draft ₹125 trade budget. If the session has already lost ₹300 against its draft ₹350 stop, only ₹50 of daily loss room remains. Reduce size until price risk plus costs and slippage fits ₹50, or pass. Reserve that room for pending fills. Trigger the daily halt on realised plus marked open losses and charges. Stop orders can fill beyond their trigger.

**Learning between runs · proposed design.** On restart, load the last reconciled session, then fetch fresh broker state. Save the decision-time market snapshot, trade or pass, human override, original stop and exit reason. Link decisions to fills, total costs, incidents and rejected hypotheses. After close, identify whether a loss came from the thesis, execution, costs, or an unresolved cause. Review strategy-rule changes weekly, outside open positions. Preserve losing versions, log the evidence that changed the rule, and compare the next version on later sessions. A veto, forced trade or infrastructure outage must remain visible in the 90-day record.

## How much money the experiment can use

The following are **proposed limits for discussion**. Puneet has specified the ₹1 lakh trading-capital ceiling. The smaller exposure and loss limits are draft choices.

| Decision | Proposed rule |
|---|---|
| Deposit and exposure | The first live sessions may use up to ₹10,000 gross exposure. A later reviewed increase may take that to ₹25,000. The remaining ₹90,000 or ₹75,000 stays idle or outside the broker. No leverage or top-ups. |
| Position sizing | Start with ₹125 planned loss per trade, including charges and estimated slippage. Divide the available risk by the stop distance per share, round down, then apply the exposure and available-cash caps. Skip if even one share fails. |
| Repeated losses | Up to two completed or attempted entries per session. Count retries under the original intent. After two losing trades in a session, stop for that day. Three consecutive losing trades across sessions pause the strategy for review. |

| Closing positions | A confirmed fill needs matching broker-side protection. If protection fails, stop new entries and attempt to close the exposed quantity. Exit before the broker cutoff with time to retry safely. Price gaps, halts or outages can exceed every planned stop. |
| Halt and restart | Cancel pending entries, preserve working protection while reconciling fills, and verify the broker shows zero open quantity after exiting. Alert Puneet about unresolved exposure. A restart requires a reconciled ledger and owner review. |

**Daily profits.** Keep two numbers: cash still allowed to trade and profits already reserved for withdrawal. If only ₹10,000 is funded, count the remaining ₹90,000 once as designated idle capital. Unused capital stays idle until the owner approves a higher pilot allocation. At day-end after charges, reserve only cumulative available excess above the applicable funded principal. Later gains first repair losses. Reserved profits stay outside sizing even before a bank transfer clears. Settlement generally takes until the next working settlement day. Puneet handles withdrawals from settled funds. With the full ₹1 lakh funded, earning ₹2,000 reserves ₹2,000. Losing ₹3,000 the next day leaves ₹97,000 tradable. Earning ₹1,500 brings it to ₹98,500 and triggers no sweep. Total wealth is then ₹1,00,500, counting the ₹2,000 only once. Measure losses on **account equity + designated idle cash + completed withdrawals**, including unsettled broker balances once. Deduct outside operating expenses for the all-in loss test and economic return. Moving money between those buckets never creates profit.

## How the model makes and changes live decisions

Before the open, Claude Code reads the previous handoff, checks available funds and positions, selects a liquid watchlist and writes its hypotheses. For each, record what would confirm it, what would invalidate it, the expected holding time and when the information expires.

During the session, keep the approved strategy rules fixed while new evidence arrives. Wake the model on a material filing or headline and at scheduled 15-minute market checkpoints.
